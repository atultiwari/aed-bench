# Using an AED on a collapsed adult: spec shared by every style

Sketchbook (`sketchbook/`), Turntable (`turntable/`) and Working Model (`working-model/`) all follow this one file:
the same stations, the same labels, the same order and the same clock.

## Sources

**Primary: ERC 2025.** Smyth MA, van Goor S, Hansen CM, et al. *European Resuscitation Council Guidelines 2025: Adult
Basic Life Support.* Resuscitation 2025;215(Suppl 1):110771. doi:10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110771.
Cited below as **ERC-BLS**, with the part used:
- **Fig. 7, "BLS step by step"** (pp. 8–9): the order of actions and the wording of each step.
- **"Using an automated external defibrillator (AED)"** (concise guidelines, pp. 4–5): opening the case, switching on,
  pads, no touching during analysis, shock, resume compressions.
- **"How and when to use an AED"**, **"Should chest compressions be performed before defibrillation?"**, **"Positioning
  of defibrillation pads"**, **"Removing a bra prior to defibrillation"**, **"Safety"** (evidence sections, pp. 20–22).
- **Fig. 6**: anterior-lateral (a) and anterior-posterior (b) pad positions.
- **Table 1**: the 2025 change: call the emergency number for anyone unresponsive, then check breathing.

**Primary: ERC 2025 Adult ALS** (only for the numbers BLS does not give). Soar J, Böttiger BW, et al. *ERC Guidelines 2025:
Adult Advanced Life Support.* Resuscitation 2025;215:110769. Cited as **ERC-ALS**: "Defibrillation strategy" (single
shock, resume compressions at once, 2-minute cycle, pulse often absent for more than 2 min after a successful shock);
"Energy levels".

**Cross-check: AHA 2025.** *Part 7: Adult Basic Life Support. 2025 AHA Guidelines for CPR and ECC.* Circulation 2025.
doi:10.1161/CIR.0000000000001369. Cited as **AHA-P7** with the recommendation table and number, e.g.
"AHA-P7, Pads for Defibrillation #1". "AHA-P7 compressions" is the table of rate, depth and recoil, which the
cpr.heart.org page mistakenly captions a second "Fundamentals of Ventilation". Where ERC and AHA agree, both are cited; where only one says it, that one.

"**Typical**" = not given by either guideline; a common value in AED training and devices. These are the numbers
to check.

## Who is on the floor

Two rescuers, because that is how most workshops teach it and both guidelines describe it:
- **Rescuer A** finds the person and does the compressions throughout.
- **Rescuer B** fetches the AED and operates it while A keeps pushing.

ERC-BLS: "If more than one rescuer is present, continue CPR while the defibrillation pads are being attached."
A lone rescuer does the same steps alone (a note on step 2 says so). The person is assumed to be in **ventricular
fibrillation (VF)**, so the AED will advise a shock. That is a teaching scenario; the "no shock advised" branch is
covered in a note and a wrong-order message.

## Stations (18, each visited once, in order; the active hand only moves forward)

The "carrier" is the active rescuer's hand (a cursor ring on the drawing). Rescuer A stays on the chest from station 7
to the end; the active station is whatever the step is about.

| # | Label on the drawing | Full name (active card) | Scenario time | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | START | A person collapses: an adult lying still on the floor | 0 s | – |
| 2 | SAFE? | Check it is safe: traffic, water, electricity, danger to you | 3 s | ERC-BLS Fig. 7 "Safety"; time typical |
| 3 | SHOULDERS | Check for a response: shake the shoulders, ask loudly "Are you all right?" | 5 s | ERC-BLS Fig. 7 "Response"; time typical |
| 4 | CALL 108 / 112 | Call the emergency number on speaker (India: 108 ambulance or 112; Europe: 112) | 5 s (call stays open) | ERC-BLS Fig. 7 "Alert emergency services"; Table 1 (call before the breathing check); AHA-P7 Initiation (Lay) #2–3 |
| 5 | HEAD + CHIN | Open the airway (head tilt, chin lift) and look, listen, feel for breathing | **10 s (no more)** | **ERC-BLS Fig. 7 "Airway", "Breathing": no more than 10 s**; AHA-P7 Recognition #1 |
| 6 | HELPER | Send someone for the AED | 2 s | ERC-BLS Fig. 7 "Send for AED"; time typical |
| 7 | CHEST | CPR while the AED comes: 30 compressions, 2 breaths | 1 min 30 s | ERC-BLS Fig. 7 "Circulation", "Combine rescue breathing"; AHA-P7 CPR Before Defibrillation #1; **time is illustrative** (depends how far the AED is) |
| 8 | LID | Open the AED case or lid | 3 s | ERC-BLS "Using an AED": "Open the AED case (if present)"; time typical |
| 9 | ON | Power button (some AEDs switch on when the lid opens) | 2 s | ERC-BLS "Using an AED": "Some AEDs automatically turn on… If not, identify the power button"; time typical |
| 10 | BARE CHEST | Bare the chest and open the pad packet (move a bra rather than remove it, if pads can sit on bare skin) | 10 s | ERC-BLS "Removing a bra"; AHA-P7 Pads #2; time typical |
| 11 | PAD 1 | Right pad: below the right collarbone, right of the breastbone | 5 s | **ERC-BLS "Positioning of defibrillation pads"**, Fig. 6a; AHA-P7 Pads #1 |
| 12 | PAD 2 | Left pad: left side, mid-axillary line, below the armpit | 5 s | **ERC-BLS "Positioning"**, Fig. 6a; AHA-P7 Pads #1 |
| 13 | PLUG | Pad connector into the AED (many come pre-connected) | 2 s | Typical (manufacturer); ERC-BLS: follow the AED's prompts |
| 14 | ANALYSING | AED analyses the rhythm: nobody touches the person | 8 s | **ERC-BLS "Using an AED": "Ensure that nobody touches the person whilst the AED is analysing"**; time typical (device-dependent) |
| 15 | STAND CLEAR | "Shock advised": AED charges; look along the body, call "Everyone clear!" | 6 s | ERC-BLS Fig. 7 "Follow the spoken/visual directions"; charge time typical |
| 16 | SHOCK | Press the flashing shock button (a fully automatic AED shocks by itself) | 1 s | **ERC-BLS "Using an AED"** (semi- vs fully automatic); energy set by the device, see Numbers |
| 17 | CHEST | Resume CPR immediately: 2 minutes of 30:2 | **2 min** | **ERC-BLS Fig. 7: "Then immediately resume CPR"**; **ERC-ALS "Continue CPR for 2 min until rhythm reanalysis"**; AHA-P7 Pauses #3 |
| 18 | NEXT CHECK | AED says "Stop CPR, analysing": swap compressors, repeat | – | ERC-BLS "after a set time interval the AED will instruct the rescuer to pause CPR"; AHA-P7 Pauses #4 (swap about every 2 min) |

**Scenario clock total: 4 min 37 s** from collapse to the second analysis (must read the same on all three pages).
Split: check and call 25 s · CPR waiting for the AED 1 min 30 s · AED on and pads 27 s (CPR continues) · hands-off
around the shock 15 s · CPR after the shock 2 min.

## Steps (7 buttons)

| Step | Stations | Note: what is happening (plain words, real terms) | Done line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Check and call | 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 | Make sure it is safe, shake and shout. No answer: call for an ambulance on speaker straight away (in India 108 or 112), then tilt the head and look for normal breathing for up to 10 s. Gasping is **not** normal breathing: it is a sign of cardiac arrest. Send someone for the AED. | Unresponsive, not breathing normally: cardiac arrest. Help and an AED are coming. |
| 2. Start CPR | 7 | Heel of the hand on the lower half of the breastbone, other hand on top, arms straight. Push 5–6 cm deep, 100–120 a minute, let the chest come all the way up. If trained: 30 pushes, 2 breaths. If not: keep pushing without stopping. Alone? Fetch an AED only if you can have it on within 1 minute. | Blood is being pushed to the brain while the AED is on its way. |
| 3. Open and switch on | 8 → 9 | B opens the lid and presses the power button; the AED starts talking. A keeps pushing: do not stop CPR to set up the AED. | The AED is on and giving spoken instructions. |
| 4. Attach the pads | 10 → 11 → 12 → 13 | Bare the chest (move clothing or a bra; modesty comes second). Peel each pad and press it on bare skin exactly where the picture on the pad shows: one below the right collarbone, one on the left side below the armpit. A keeps pushing around B's hands. | Pads on, plugged in. The AED can now see the heart. |
| 5. Analyse: hands off | 14 | The AED says "Analysing, do not touch the patient". A lifts both hands. Any movement can confuse the analysis. | Rhythm read: ventricular fibrillation. "Shock advised." |
| 6. Stand clear and shock | 15 → 16 | The AED charges. B looks from head to toe and says "Everyone clear!", then presses the flashing button. The body may jerk. Keep this pause as short as you can. | Shock delivered. |
| 7. Resume CPR at once | 17 → 18 | Straight back to compressions: don't feel for a pulse, don't wait. Even a successful shock leaves a heart that cannot pump well for a while, often for more than 2 minutes. After 2 minutes the AED will ask to analyse again; swap who is pushing. Keep going until help takes over, or the person wakes, moves, opens their eyes and breathes normally. | CPR running. Next analysis in 2 minutes. |

Clinician lines (shown under the note, smaller, labelled "For clinicians"):
- Step 1: "Healthcare professionals may check a carotid pulse at the same time, for no more than 10 s; if no definite
  pulse, treat as cardiac arrest." (AHA-P7 Recognition #2)
- Step 2: "Breaths over about 1 s each, just enough for visible chest rise; pause no more than 10 s for two breaths."
  (ERC-BLS Fig. 7; AHA-P7 Ventilation #2, #4)
- Step 6: "Pre- and post-shock pauses should be as short as possible." (AHA-P7 Pauses #1). With a manual defibrillator,
  ERC-ALS aims for an interruption under 5 s; an AED's own analysis and charge set the pause here.
- Step 7: "No post-shock rhythm or pulse check; resume compressions immediately." (AHA-P7 Pauses #3; ERC-ALS)

## Numbers (every number on any page)

| Number | Value used | Range in the source | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breathing check | 10 s | no more than 10 s | ERC-BLS Fig. 7 |
| Compression rate | **110 / min** | 100–120 / min | ERC-BLS Fig. 7; AHA-P7 compressions #3. 110 chosen inside the range |
| Compression depth | 5–6 cm (shown as a band) | at least 5 cm, not more than 6 cm | ERC-BLS Fig. 7; AHA-P7 compressions #1 |
| Compressions : breaths | 30 : 2 (or continuous if untrained) | – | ERC-BLS "Rescue breaths"; AHA-P7 Ratio #1 |
| Pause for 2 breaths | **5 s** used; never more than 10 s | ≤ 10 s; each breath about 1 s | ERC-BLS Fig. 7; 5 s typical |
| One 30:2 cycle | 30 / 110 min = 16.4 s + 5 s = **21.4 s** | – | computed |
| Cycles in 2 min after the shock | 5.6 ("about 5") | AHA: "about 5 cycles" ≈ 2 min | computed; AHA-P7 Pauses #4 |
| CPR while the AED comes | 1 min 30 s | – | **illustrative scenario value** |
| Time to fetch an AED alone | ≤ 1 min, else start CPR | – | ERC-BLS Fig. 7 "Send for AED" |
| AED analysis | 8 s | device-dependent | **typical** |
| Charge after "shock advised" | 5 s within the 6 s at STAND CLEAR | device-dependent | **typical** |
| Hands-off around the shock | 15 s (analyse 8 + clear and charge 6 + shock 1) | "as short as possible" | AHA-P7 Pauses #1; value is the sum of typical times |
| Shock energy | "Set by the AED" on the drawing; the card says "usually 150–200 J biphasic for adults" | ERC-ALS: biphasic first shock ≥ 150 J (pulsed biphasic 130–150 J) | ERC-ALS "Energy levels"; AED value typical |
| CPR before the next analysis | 2 min | 2 min | ERC-ALS "Defibrillation strategy" |
| Swap compressors | every 2 min | about every 2 min | AHA-P7 Pauses #4 |
| Pad size | not shown | adult electrode > 8 cm | AHA-P7 Pads #1 (left out: lay users cannot choose) |

The Working Model has one toggle, **Breaths: 30:2 / compressions only**, which changes the compression counter and the
cycle length (continuous: 110 / min, no pauses); the scenario clock stays the same.

## The window

Two linked panels on the right (laptop) or one inset with a tab (phone):

**A. The heart** (cut-away, front view, pads drawn on the skin outline above it).
**B. The AED screen strip** (the trace the AED "sees"; most public AEDs show no trace, so it is labelled
"what the AED is reading").

| After step | Heart | AED strip |
|---|---|---|
| start | Quivering: chaotic purple-red ripples, no squeeze, chambers full and still (VF) | (AED not yet here) blank |
| 1 | Same quiver; a small brain icon greys out with a timer "no blood flow: 0:25" | blank |
| 2 | Quiver continues, but each push squeezes the heart between breastbone and spine: blue/red blood arrows pulse at 110 / min; brain icon partly re-coloured | blank |
| 3 | Same as 2 | Screen lights: "Attach pads to patient's bare chest" |
| 4 | Same as 2; pads appear on the outline, a faint dashed line pad-to-pad passes through the heart | Noisy CPR artefact (big regular bumps) |
| 5 | Compressions stop: arrows fade; pure quiver | Fine chaotic VF wiggle; "Analysing…" bar fills over 8 s |
| 6 | Bright yellow current flash along the pad-to-pad line through the whole heart; ripples stop at once | Charge bar, then a single tall spike; "Shock delivered" |
| 7 | Heart still and not yet pumping well ("stunned"); pushes move blood again; a soft note: "a shock stops the chaos; CPR keeps blood moving until the heart pumps on its own" | CPR artefact; a 2:00 countdown to the next analysis |

Named states each style blends between: `vf`, `vf+cpr`, `paused-vf`, `current`, `stunned+cpr`.
No step shows a pulse coming back: the guideline says it is rare to feel one straight after a shock (ERC-ALS), and the
figure must not suggest the shock "restarts" the heart.

## Wrong-order presses

| Pressed too early | What the page says |
|---|---|
| Start CPR (2) before Check and call (1) | "First make sure it's safe and call 108 or 112: the call-taker can coach you through CPR." |
| Open and switch on (3) before Start CPR (2) | "Start pushing first. CPR keeps the brain alive while the AED is fetched; your helper sets it up." |
| Attach the pads (4) before switching on (3) | "Switch the AED on first: its voice tells you where each pad goes." |
| Analyse (5) before pads (4) | "The AED can only read the heart through the pads: attach them first." |
| Shock (6) before Analyse (5) | "Only the AED decides if a shock is needed. The shock button lights up only after it says 'Shock advised'." |
| Pressing the patient (chest) during step 5 | "Hands off while it analyses: movement confuses the rhythm reading." |
| Resume CPR (7) before Shock (6) | "A shock was advised: give it now. Don't delay a shock for more CPR." (ERC-BLS: "Defibrillation should not be delayed to provide additional CPR.") |

"No shock advised" (mentioned in step 5's More text, not played): "Then restart compressions immediately and follow
the AED: it will check again in 2 minutes." (ERC-BLS Fig. 7 "If no shock is advised")

## Big idea

**The AED decides; you keep the blood moving.** Push hard and fast until it's on, hands off only while it reads and
shocks, then push again at once.

## Words we use (one name per thing on every page)

AED (first use: "automated external defibrillator") · pads (not "electrodes" or "paddles") · shock (not "zap") ·
compressions / pushing.

**Emergency number.** The phone on the drawing reads **108 / 112**, and the call is written as "call 108 or 112"
everywhere:
- **108**: the emergency ambulance number most people in India know and use (state ambulance services).
- **112**: India's single emergency number (police, fire, ambulance), and the European emergency number used in the
  ERC guidelines.
- The note on the phone adds: "Elsewhere, call your local emergency number (911 in the US and Canada)."

Either number works in India. Use the one your area answers fastest. This is local practice, not something either
guideline specifies: ERC-BLS says "call your local emergency number".

## Safety line

Under the notes on every page, small: "A teaching figure, not a substitute for a CPR course. In a real emergency, call
your emergency number and follow the AED's voice." (Typical practice for teaching material; the guidelines advise
training but do not prescribe this text.)
